BOOTCAMP 1.0

BASIC CREW TRAINING

A practical introduction to operating together aboard our capital ship,
on the ground, and as a unified crew.

◎ Objective

Build a common foundation for all JMBN members — how we operate, communicate and work together, from ship to ground.

☑ Outcome

By the end of Bootcamp 1.0, you will:

  • Understand how JMBN operates
  • Know what is expected of you
  • Operate effectively in a ground team
  • Function as part of a Polaris crew
  • Communicate and coordinate with the wider crew

ⓘ Details

  • ♟Open to all members
    (New and existing)
  • ◷~ 2.5–3 hours
  • ⌖Aboard the JMBN Polaris
    (Training Session)
  • ◇Casual Milsim / Practical Training
    (No pass or fail)

▣ Training Programme

Induction & Etiquette

◷ ≈ 20 min

  • Punctuality & preparation (be ready, we won’t wait)
  • Communication etiquette (clear, concise, relevant)
  • Teamwork, initiative and expectations

Ground Coordination

◷ ≈ 45 min

  • Friendly identification (avoid friendly fire)
  • Casualty recovery (revive and extract)

03 — Capital Ship Coordination · Training Reference

03.01 — Medical Registration

First action upon boarding: set your regeneration point at the Polaris Medbay. Confirm it is registered before proceeding.

03.02 — Ship Familiarisation

Locate the bridge, Medbay, engineering, hangar, cargo bay and access points.

03.03 — Station Familiarisation

Know your assigned station, how to reach it and your responsibilities. Practise reporting to it when the XO announces a condition change.

03.04 — Condition Levels

All condition changes are managed and announced by the Executive Officer (XO), preceded by the boatswain’s whistle.

CONDITION 1

Underway — Danger Confirmed
  • 100% readiness; all crew man battle stations.
  • Formal stance and communications.
  • No personal breaks or AFK; movement limited to situational response.
  • Master Mode: SCM.
  • If the ship has missiles, load two for quick launch.

CONDITION 2

Underway — Potential Danger
  • 100% readiness; all crew man battle stations.
  • Formal stance and communications.
  • Last chance for personal breaks: one person per division at a time.
  • Movement limited to situational response and sustenance.
  • Master Mode: SCM.

CONDITION 3

Underway — Peacetime
  • Minimum readiness at key watch positions and weapons.
  • Casual stance and communications.
  • At least one person per division on station.
  • Free movement for sustenance and personal logistics.
  • Master Mode: NAV.

CONDITION 4

In Port — Fully Landed / Docked
  • All weapons powered down.
  • Casual stance and communications.
  • Await instructions from division supervisors or officers.
  • Officers and chiefs convene at the wardroom.

Practical readiness drill: Start at Condition 4. The XO announces different condition levels; crew demonstrate where to report, what duties to perform, when movement is permitted and what communication etiquette applies.

03.05 — Nested Landing & Takeoff

Clearance request: “[Ship Name], [Own Callsign], requesting Green Deck.”

GREEN DECK

Cleared for landing and takeoff
  • Landing: cleared to embark and land inside the hangar. For multiple craft, flight leaders determine landing order and land last.
  • Takeoff: cleared to launch safely; wait for hangar doors to open.

AMBER DECK

Quick landing / prepare for imminent takeoff
  • Landing: cleared to land inside or on top of the hangar bay doors. Flight leaders determine order and land last.
  • When embarked: prepare personnel, logistics and repairs for imminent launch. Do not launch until Green Deck is granted.

RED DECK

Not cleared for landing or takeoff — danger present
  • Landing: do not land or embark; a threat endangers the host ship.
  • Takeoff: do not launch, whether inside the hangar or resting on its doors.

All craft must request Green Deck from the host ship. Green and Amber Deck may be authorised by the Captain, XO or Tactical Action Officer on their behalf. The Tactical Action Officer may declare Red Deck at their discretion.

03.06 — Ship Security Awareness

Recognise and report unauthorised personnel or suspicious activity. Detailed anti-boarding drills are reserved for specialised training.
